## 1. Executive Summary

NetSpout Gate 10.5 executes a bounded remediation and semantic closure across the five Wave 2 operational scenarios evaluated during Gate 10 independent product acceptance. Gate 10 confirmed that NetSpout enables engineers to investigate and prove complex carrier, wireless, and enterprise operational problems in Splunk without owning physical infrastructure. However, independent acceptance surfaced three P2 friction findings regarding protocol convergence timing truth and wireless terminology, and one P3 configuration friction finding:

1. **`W2-P2-001` (Service Provider TI-LFA Timing Truth & Routing Semantics):** Sub-50ms fast reroute was modeled in simulation logic (`convergence_ms=32`) but lacked an explicit declarative `MODELED` timing badge in the UI and documentation.
2. **`W2-P2-002` (Carrier Ring G.8032 ERPS Timing Truth & WTR Distinction):** Sub-50ms ring protection (`unblocked in 38ms`) was modeled in simulation logic and required explicit distinction between sub-50ms failover and the Wait-to-Restore (WTR) post-repair stabilization hold timer.
3. **`W2-P2-003` (Wireless CleanAir DCA vs DFS Semantics):** In `arch_wlan_meraki_catalyst`, the signature text referenced Dynamic Frequency Selection (DFS) for non-Wi-Fi RF interference. In IEEE 802.11 standards, DFS applies strictly to radar pulse detection on channels 52–144, whereas non-Wi-Fi interference mitigation is executed by Dynamic Channel Assignment (DCA) / Radio Resource Management (RRM).
4. **`W2-P3-001` (Development Pipeline TLS Default):** Self-signed TLS was defaulted to disabled, causing connection friction on local Docker Splunk deployments (`127.0.0.1:8888`).

Gate 10.5 introduces a reusable, declarative timing metadata model across the canonical core and UI, cleanses protocol and wireless semantics, promotes the two clean Gate 10 scenarios (`arch_lan_campus_access` and `arch_vpn_remote_workforce`), retests the three remediated scenarios to clean PASS, and promotes them to `GOLDEN_PATH_CERTIFIED`.

## 2. Declarative Timing Evidence Model

### 2.1. Classification Definitions

To prevent misleading ambiguity between empirical hardware packet measurements and simulation logic, NetSpout establishes a strict four-state timing taxonomy:

| Classification | Definition | Evidence Requirement | Example in NetSpout |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`MEASURED`** | Elapsed duration empirically calculated from observed packet arrivals or hardware counters. | Calculated from two distinct timestamps (\(T_1 - T_0\)) or microsecond ASIC telemetry. | Hardware test-bench packet captures. |
| **`MODELED`** | Deterministically generated by NetSpout's protocol simulation engine according to industry standards. | Structured numeric field in syslog/telemetry payload (`convergence_ms=32`, `unblocked in 38ms`). | Cisco IOS-XR TI-LFA local repair, Nokia SR-OS G.8032 ERPS RPL unblock. |
| **`DECLARED_ONLY`** | Textual claim appearing only in scenario documentation or descriptions without structured field modeling. | Qualitative string in message body or markdown description. | General scenario narrative descriptions. |
| **`NOT_APPLICABLE`** | Scenario does not make a quantitative duration or convergence claim. | Standard event/metric telemetry without sub-50ms or fast-failover assertions. | Campus 802.1X access, AnyConnect SSL-VPN auth. |

### 2.2. Schema Extensions

The timing metadata schema is incorporated directly into canonical models:

- `src/netspout_core/models.py` (`ScenarioContract`, `UseCaseContract`, `RunManifest`):
  ```python
  timing_claim: Optional[str] = None
  timing_value: Optional[float] = None
  timing_unit: Optional[str] = None
  timing_classification: str = "NOT_APPLICABLE"  # MEASURED, MODELED, DECLARED_ONLY, NOT_APPLICABLE
  timing_notes: Optional[str] = None
  ```
- Replicated deterministically to `netspout/bin/` and `backend/app/` via `scripts/sync_core.py`.
- Exposed in `/api/use-cases` and mapped to `frontend/src/types/workflow.ts` (`UseCase` interface).

## 3. Remediated Scenarios & Technical Semantics

### 3.1. Service Provider Core Routing (`service_provider_cisco`)

- **Timing Metadata:**
  - `timing_claim`: `"TI-LFA Fast Reroute"`
  - `timing_value`: `32.0`
  - `timing_unit`: `"ms"`
  - `timing_classification`: `"MODELED"`
  - `timing_notes`: `"Modeled sub-50ms local data-plane protection prior to BGP control-plane convergence. NetSpout models this protocol behavior and does not claim empirical hardware packet measurements."`
- **Routing Protocol Causality:**
  1. *Physical / Link Failure:* Upstream carrier circuit HundredGigE0/0/0/1 flaps.
  2. *BGP Session Drop:* eBGP hold timer expires, adjacency to peer `198.51.100.1` transitions to Down (`%ROUTING-BGP-5-ADJCHANGE`).
  3. *Core Route Withdrawal:* Prefix withdrawal propagates downstream across PE routers (`%ROUTING-BGP-5-ADJCHANGE`).
  4. *IGP / TI-LFA Local Repair:* Topology-Independent Loop-Free Alternate engages pre-computed backup path HundredGigE0/0/0/2 in 32ms (`%MPLS-6-TI_LFA_LOCAL_REPAIR`). Crucially, TI-LFA operates in the data-plane immediately and does not wait for BGP control-plane convergence.
  5. *Stabilization & BGP Peering Restored:* Carrier transport stabilizes; eBGP session re-establishes (`%ROUTING-BGP-5-ADJCHANGE neighbor Up`).
  6. *Equilibrium:* Routing table reaches equilibrium and fast-reroute disarms (`%ROUTING-BGP-6-STABILITY`).

### 3.2. Metro Carrier Ethernet Ring (`arch_man_carrier_ring`)

- **Timing Metadata:**
  - `timing_claim`: `"ITU-T G.8032 ERPS Protection"`
  - `timing_value`: `38.0`
  - `timing_unit`: `"ms"`
  - `timing_classification`: `"MODELED"`
  - `timing_notes`: `"Modeled sub-50ms RPL unblock failover. WTR is a post-repair stabilization hold timer before revertive restoration, distinct from protection switching time."`
- **G.8032 ERPS Protocol Causality:**
  1. *Baseline IDLE:* Ring operates normally with Ring Protection Link (RPL) blocked on Node A to prevent Layer 2 loops (`%ETH_RING-6-INFO state=RING_IDLE`).
  2. *Signal Fail (SF):* Physical terrestrial fiber cut on 100G span `1/1/c1` between Node A and Node B triggers immediate optical LOS (`%ETH_RING-4-SIGNAL_FAILURE`).
  3. *R-APS Broadcast:* Ring nodes circulate R-APS Signal Fail control frames across healthy spans in < 5ms (`juniper:junos`).
  4. *RPL Unblock (Protection Switching):* Node A (RPL Owner) unblocks the RPL port in 38ms, restoring transit forwarding around the healthy arc (`%ETH_RING-4-RPL_UNBLOCK`).
  5. *Wait-to-Restore (WTR) Hold:* Fiber span is physically spliced; Node A starts the G.8032 WTR hold timer (typically 5 minutes, modeled deterministically) to prevent flap oscillation.
  6. *Revertive Restoration:* Upon WTR expiry, the ring reverts to nominal IDLE topology with the RPL re-blocked (`%ETH_RING-5-REVERTIVE_RESTORE`).

### 3.3. Wireless CleanAir & Air Marshal (`arch_wlan_meraki_catalyst`)

- **Semantic Correction:** Eliminated the term Dynamic Frequency Selection (DFS) from non-Wi-Fi interference mitigation.
- **Updated Signature:** `"CleanAir Dynamic Channel Assignment (DCA) Reassignment"`
- **RF Causality & Separation of Concerns:**
  1. *RF Non-Wi-Fi Interference:* Continuous industrial frequency jammer/video bridge elevates noise floor to -58dBm and utilization to 94.5% on Channel 36 (`%DOT11-4-CLEANAIR_INTERFERENCE`).
  2. *Radar Detection Clarification:* Zero radar signatures detected; DFS is not triggered.
  3. *Automated Dynamic Channel Assignment:* Catalyst CleanAir RRM shifts AP radio from congested Channel 36 to pristine Channel 100 (`%DOT11-5-CLEANAIR_CHANNEL_SWITCH ... via automated DCA/RRM`).
  4. *Rogue AP Evil-Twin Broadcast:* Adversary broadcasts spoofed corporate SSID `"Corp-Executive-Secure"` on BSSID `00:14:22:01:23:45` (`%CATALYST_SEC-4-ROGUE_ALERT`, `alertType="air_marshal_rogue_detected"`).
  5. *Air Marshal Containment:* Meraki sensor and Catalyst WLC enforce 802.11 deauthentication matrix containment (`%AIRMARSHAL-4-ROGUE_CONTAINMENT`).
  6. *RF Restoration:* Airspace normalizes on Channel 100 with 14.2% utilization and 38dB client SNR (`%DOT11-6-CLEANAIR_HEALTH`).
